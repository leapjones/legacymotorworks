How to buy a used Spec E46

By Peter Jones, Legacy Motorworks · PUBLISH_DATE
Short answer

Find out what's fresh and what isn't, who built the cage, and what the logbook shows. Then get eyes on the chassis. Most problems with a used race car live in what the listing doesn't say.

The listing tells you what the seller wants you to know. The car tells you the rest.

I build cars and I sell cars. My own cars are always for sale, and several of my clients race one now. So I've written plenty of listings and read a lot more of them. The good ones say what's used as plainly as what's new. Most don't.

Here's how I'd go about it.

Ask what isn't fresh

Everybody lists the new parts. Ask for the other list: what's original, what's aging, and how many race weekends it's been on the car. If the answer is vague, the car probably is too.

Ask who built the cage

The cage is the hardest thing on the car to fix later. A cage from a nationally respected builder is a big part of why a car resells. Ask for the name. If nobody knows, that's an answer.

Look at the chassis yourself

Some damage shows. Bad paint in the wheel arches. Damaged frame horns. Cracked seam sealer in the wheel well aprons. Any of those tells you the car's been hit.

Some doesn't show. A chassis that's 1/16 of an inch out [PJ: confirm before publish] won't show to the naked eye. On a build it can surface at alignment, at the very end. On a used car it surfaces after you've paid.

Look at what's been fitted for feel

Not every part that improves FEEL is worth it. Some of them move the load somewhere you'd rather it didn't go. I wrote up the one I'd look for first: solid control arm bushings.

Price it against the weekend

Used cars run $35K to $65K. A car at the low end that needs work before it's safe to race isn't the cheap one. It's pay now or pay later, and later usually comes with a lost weekend attached.

What a checklist can't do

A checklist and a phone call can't replace hands on the car. If you can't get there yourself, get someone you trust to look at it before money moves. At the end of the day you're buying the car, not the listing.
